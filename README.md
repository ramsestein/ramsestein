I'm an anesthesiologist, but I spend far more time in the surgical ICU at **Hospital Clínic de Barcelona** than in the operating room. My day is split between those beds and the hospital's AI Laboratory, where I coordinate clinical AI — mostly the unglamorous half: getting a model from a script in my terminal to a bedside monitor without breaking anything. Or anyone. Right now I'm also an **AI research fellow at SIMTRI, Seoul National University Hospital** 🇰🇷, which has added nine time zones to my list of confounders. CPHIMS certified, which mainly means I've read the standards so my colleagues don't have to.

Everything in here comes from the same recurring problem: clinical data is messy, private, and rarely behaves the way the paper promised.

<br>

## 🔬 What I'm into

> **Physiological signals, raw** — half a kilohertz per bed, day and night, in my own ICU. A good chunk of what's here exists only to audit those files and confirm they aren't just noise with a timestamp. Yes, I have opinions about hard drives now.

> **Clinical NLP in Spanish & Catalan** — de-identification pipelines and synthetic corpora, because you can't do research on notes you're not allowed to read.

> **Model assurance** — calibration, drift and domain adaptation. A model trained in Seoul does not automatically speak Barcelona.

> **AI governance** — ethics protocols and bias audits: the paperwork standing between a lovely demo and an actual patient.
